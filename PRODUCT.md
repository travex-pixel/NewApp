# Instinct

## Purpose

A social judgement game. Five everyday situations, four plausible responses each, a verdict with what the choice gets you and what it costs, and after five, a social aura: an affectionate, slightly roasting archetype drawn from the pattern of your answers. A social experience disguised as a tiny game.

It is not a clinical test, a personality quiz or a self-improvement app. It never claims a single correct answer or a measurement of a person.

## Audience

Anyone arriving from a social link on a phone with thirty seconds of patience: friends, mums, aunties, colleagues. Broad, adult, English-speaking, across cultures; situations are chosen to be lived-through by most people (family groups, dinners, flatmates, work, neighbours, dating).

## Operating context

- Phone first, in-app browsers included. The situation and reveal never scroll at 390×844.
- No account, no email, no onboarding. The first decision arrives within seconds.
- Progress is device-local. Today's five is the same for everyone (UTC), so results are comparable in a group chat.
- Sharing is real: PNG card via native share, download, copy link, copy text; per-situation challenge links.
- No LLM at runtime. Authored content and deterministic scoring only.

## Constraints

- Next.js App Router on Vercel, solo-founder budget, no paid infrastructure.
- Content is the product. Every verdict is specific to its answer; every path has a real upside and a real downside.
- No fake social proof, no fake scores, no streaks, no energy systems.

## Voice

An observant, funny friend. Specific, warm, dry. The roast lands on the behaviour, never the person. Sendable to a mum.
